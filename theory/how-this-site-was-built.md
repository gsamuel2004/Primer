<h2>About the Communication Theory Primer</h2>

<p>This website was developed as a communication theory primer exploring how artificial intelligence influences the ways people create, interpret, and understand messages. Its purpose is to make foundational communication theories accessible to readers who may not have a background in communication studies.</p>

<p>Rather than presenting communication theory only as an academic subject, this project connects established theoretical perspectives to everyday experiences, including professional emails, workplace conversations, social media interactions, and AI-assisted writing.</p>

<h2>The Central Question</h2>

<p>The central question guiding this primer is: <strong>What happens to meaning, intention, and trust when artificial intelligence helps write our words?</strong></p>

<p>AI systems can produce messages that are grammatically correct, professionally structured, and technically effective. However, successful communication involves more than delivering information. It also depends on cultural expectations, relationships, interpretation, and the intentions associated with a message.</p>

<p>This project explores those complications by connecting classical communication theories to contemporary AI-assisted communication.</p>

<h2>Theoretical Foundations</h2>

<p>The primer is organized around four major theoretical perspectives and a final section of practical guidance.</p>

<h3>1. Shannon and Weaver: The Transmission View</h3>

<p>The transmission perspective explains how messages travel through communication systems and how noise can interfere with their delivery. This primer applies that framework to AI-assisted writing to demonstrate that successfully transmitting information does not necessarily guarantee shared understanding.</p>

<h3>2. Semiotics: Meaning and Culture</h3>

<p>Drawing on Daniel Chandler's discussion of semiotics, this section examines how language operates through signs, conventions, and cultural associations. It considers why AI-generated wording may communicate unintended meanings even when the information itself is accurate.</p>

<h3>3. Stuart Hall: Interpretation and Power</h3>

<p>Hall's encoding/decoding model explains how audiences can interpret the same message through dominant, negotiated, or oppositional readings. This framework helps demonstrate why AI-assisted messages can be interpreted differently depending on the recipient's experiences and expectations.</p>

<h3>4. Umberto Eco: Interpretation and Intention</h3>

<p>Eco's framework distinguishes among the author's intention, the text's intention, and the reader's intention. This primer applies those perspectives to AI-assisted writing and proposes an additional analytical category called <em>intentio algorithmi</em>.</p>

<p>This proposed fourth perspective examines how AI systems influence the language of messages through training patterns, system instructions, and other operational factors. It does not suggest that AI possesses human intentions or consciousness.</p>

<h3>5. Reader Guidance</h3>

<p>The final practical section translates communication theory into recommendations for evaluating AI-assisted messages. It includes questions about accuracy, intention, tone, interpretation, and responsibility.</p>

<h2>How the Website Was Developed</h2>

<p>This primer was built using GitHub Pages and Jekyll, a static-site generator. The website is organized into individual content pages, allowing readers to explore each theoretical perspective separately while maintaining a consistent visual design.</p>

<p>The project uses HTML for content structure, CSS for visual presentation, and Markdown files with Jekyll front matter to organize pages and their metadata. A shared layout provides consistent navigation and styling throughout the website.</p>

<p>GitHub serves as the repository for the project's files, while GitHub Pages publishes the website online. GitHub Actions supports the automated building and deployment process.</p>

<h2>Use of Artificial Intelligence</h2>

<p>Artificial intelligence assisted with the development of this website, including the initial site structure, HTML formatting, content drafting, and technical guidance for editing and publishing pages through GitHub.</p>

<p>The project also used AI assistance to help organize communication theory explanations and develop examples connecting theoretical concepts to contemporary situations. These examples include AI-assisted workplace emails, apologies, customer service messages, and everyday interpersonal communication.</p>

<p>AI assistance was part of the project's development process rather than a substitute for responsibility for the final website. The author is responsible for reviewing the content, verifying theoretical claims and references, and ensuring that the published material accurately represents the project's purpose.</p>

<h2>Design and Accessibility</h2>

<p>The website uses a Virginia Tech-inspired color palette and a consistent visual structure to connect the project to its academic setting. Clear navigation links allow readers to move between theoretical sections, while headings and paragraphs organize the information into readable segments.</p>

<p>The HTML structure uses semantic elements such as headings, articles, and navigation components to support accessibility and clarity. The design aims to make complex communication theories approachable without removing their academic significance.</p>

<h2>Project Limitations</h2>

<p>This primer is an introductory educational resource rather than a comprehensive account of communication theory. Each section focuses on selected concepts that are especially useful for understanding AI-assisted communication.</p>

<p>The proposed concept of <em>intentio algorithmi</em> is an exploratory analytical extension introduced in this project. It should not be understood as an established concept developed by Umberto Eco or as a claim that AI systems possess human intentions.</p>

<p>The examples throughout the website are illustrative scenarios designed to demonstrate theoretical concepts. They are not presented as findings from original empirical research.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>

<p>Communication theory remains relevant because new technologies do not eliminate the human challenges of meaning, interpretation, and trust. Artificial intelligence may change how messages are produced, but it does not remove the importance of understanding how people interpret them.</p>

<p>This primer encourages readers to look beyond whether a message was successfully generated or delivered and consider the cultural, interpersonal, and technological influences that shape what it ultimately means.</p>
