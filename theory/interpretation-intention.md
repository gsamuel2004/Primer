  <h1 id="intention-title">Interpretation and Intention</h1>
  
<h2>When AI Helps Write, Whose Intention Matters?</h2>

<p>Imagine asking an AI system to help write a professional email. You provide the main points and explain what you want to communicate. The system produces a polished message with wording, structure, and expressions you might not have chosen yourself. When the recipient reads the email, whose intention shapes its meaning: yours, the text's, the reader's, or the algorithm's?</p>

<p>This question becomes increasingly important as artificial intelligence participates in everyday communication. Umberto Eco's framework offers a useful starting point for understanding interpretation, but AI-assisted writing introduces an additional influence worth examining.</p>

<h2>Umberto Eco's Three Intentions</h2>

<p>In <em>The Limits of Interpretation</em> (1990), Umberto Eco distinguishes among three perspectives for analyzing meaning: <em>intentio auctoris</em>, <em>intentio operis</em>, and <em>intentio lectoris</em>. These concepts help explain why interpreting a message involves more than identifying what its author originally wanted to say.</p>

<h3>1. Intentio Auctoris: The Author's Intention</h3>

<p><em>Intentio auctoris</em> refers to the author's intended meaning. In AI-assisted communication, this begins with the human who decides to communicate and provides instructions to the AI system.</p>

<p>For example, a salesperson might ask AI to write a follow-up email that expresses appreciation after a meeting. The salesperson's intention is to maintain a positive professional relationship and encourage further conversation.</p>

<h3>2. Intentio Operis: The Text's Intention</h3>

<p><em>Intentio operis</em> concerns the meaning supported by the text itself, including its wording, structure, and internal relationships. A text may suggest meanings that extend beyond what its author consciously intended, but interpretations still need to be supported by textual evidence.</p>

<p>In an AI-generated email, expressions such as "I sincerely appreciate your valuable time" may suggest a level of formality or emotional emphasis that the sender did not specifically request. The actual wording becomes important because the recipient encounters the finished text rather than the original prompt.</p>

<h3>3. Intentio Lectoris: The Reader's Intention</h3>

<p><em>Intentio lectoris</em> refers to the reader's interpretive aims and expectations. Readers approach communication with different experiences, assumptions, and goals, which influence how they understand a message.</p>

<p>One recipient might interpret an AI-assisted email as professional and thoughtful. Another might see the same message as generic or overly polished. These differences demonstrate how interpretation depends partly on the person receiving the communication.</p>

<h2>A Proposed Fourth Layer: Intentio Algorithmi</h2>

<p>Building on Eco's three intentions, this primer proposes a fourth analytical category: <em>intentio algorithmi</em>, or algorithmic intention. This is a proposed concept for examining how AI systems influence the production of messages even when they do not possess human intentions.</p>

<p>The term does not suggest that an AI system has personal desires, emotions, or conscious goals. Instead, it identifies the operational tendencies that shape AI-generated language, including training patterns, system instructions, optimization objectives, and the user's prompt.</p>

<p>When AI helps compose a message, these factors can influence its vocabulary, tone, structure, and level of formality. As a result, the final text may reflect both the human sender's communicative goals and the patterns of the system used to produce it.</p>

<h3>How Algorithmic Mediation Changes Communication</h3>

<p>Consider a student using AI to write an email requesting an extension from a professor. The student's intention is to explain a difficult situation and ask for additional time. The AI system might generate a highly formal message that emphasizes professionalism and politeness.</p>

<p>Although the email accurately communicates the request, its language may differ from the student's usual writing style. The professor might interpret that difference as respect, distance, or evidence of AI assistance.</p>

<p>In this example, the algorithm does not independently decide what the student wants. However, it participates in shaping how the student's intention appears in the final message.</p>

<h2>Comparing the Four Perspectives</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Intentio auctoris:</strong> What did the human sender intend to communicate?</li>
<li><strong>Intentio operis:</strong> What meanings are supported by the actual wording and structure of the message?</li>
<li><strong>Intentio lectoris:</strong> How does the recipient's perspective influence interpretation?</li>
<li><strong>Intentio algorithmi:</strong> How did the AI system's design, instructions, and learned patterns influence the message's final form?</li>
</ul>

<p>These four perspectives offer a practical way to examine communication in which humans and AI systems jointly shape the words that others encounter.</p>

<h2>Applying the Framework to Everyday Life</h2>

<p>Imagine sending an AI-assisted text message to a friend after an argument. Your goal is to apologize sincerely. The AI system suggests calm, carefully structured language. Your friend receives the message but notices that it sounds different from your usual communication style.</p>

<p>From the perspective of <em>intentio auctoris</em>, your intention was reconciliation. From <em>intentio operis</em>, the text communicates an apology through its actual wording. From <em>intentio lectoris</em>, your friend may interpret the message as sincere or impersonal. From the proposed <em>intentio algorithmi</em> perspective, the AI system's tendency to produce polished, conventional language helped shape the final expression.</p>

<p>Considering all four perspectives helps explain why a message can accurately express someone's goals while still creating an unexpected reaction.</p>

<h2>Why This Matters</h2>

<p>AI-assisted writing complicates the relationship between authorship and expression. A person may remain responsible for sending a message while relying on technology to determine how that message sounds. This makes it increasingly important to distinguish between human intention, textual meaning, reader interpretation, and algorithmic influence.</p>

<p>The proposed concept of <em>intentio algorithmi</em> is not presented as an established fourth category developed by Eco. Rather, it is an analytical extension offered by this primer to encourage further discussion about AI-mediated communication.</p>

<h2>Key Takeaway</h2>

<p><strong>When AI helps write our words, understanding communication requires examining both human intention and algorithmic influence.</strong> Eco's framework reminds us that meaning cannot be reduced to what an author wanted to say. The proposed fourth perspective invites us to consider how technology participates in shaping the messages we send and interpret.</p>
