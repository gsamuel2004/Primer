  <h1 id="transmission-title">The Transmission View and Its Limits</h1>
 <h2>When a Message Arrives but Its Meaning Does Not</h2>
<p>Imagine a salesperson using artificial intelligence to transform a few bullet points into a polished follow-up email. The email reaches the customer without any technical problems. Its grammar is correct, its structure is professional, and its information is clear. Yet the recipient may still interpret the message as impersonal or automated. This situation illustrates a central problem in AI-assisted communication: successfully delivering information does not necessarily mean successfully communicating human intention.</p>

<h2>Shannon and Weaver's Three Levels of Communication</h2>
<p>In <em>The Mathematical Theory of Communication</em> (1949), Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver provide a framework for examining communication problems at three levels.</p>

<h3>Level A: The Technical Problem</h3>
<p>The technical level asks how accurately symbols can be transmitted from a sender to a receiver. In AI-assisted communication, this level is often successful. An AI-drafted email can reach its intended recipient without errors or interruptions.</p>

<h3>Level B: The Semantic Problem</h3>
<p>The semantic level concerns how accurately transmitted symbols convey the intended meaning. AI complicates this process because the person initiating the message and the system generating its wording are not the same. A sender might intend to sound friendly, while the AI produces language that the recipient interprets as distant or overly formal.</p>

<h3>Level C: The Effectiveness Problem</h3>
<p>The effectiveness level asks whether the received meaning influences the recipient in the desired way. A perfectly delivered and understandable message may still fail if the recipient questions its sincerity. For example, a customer who suspects a sales email was generated automatically might be less inclined to respond, even if the message contains useful information.</p>

<h2>Where the Transmission View Reaches Its Limits</h2>
<p>The transmission perspective helps explain how information moves between people, but AI-assisted messaging raises questions that cannot be answered through technical accuracy alone. When a human supplies the original idea and an algorithm produces the final wording, the relationship between authorship, intention, and interpretation becomes more complicated.</p>
<p>In his 2023 essay <em>ChatGPT Is a Blurry JPEG of the Web</em>, Ted Chiang compares large language models to lossy compression systems that reproduce patterns from their training material without preserving every detail. His argument helps explain why grammatically polished AI-generated writing should not automatically be mistaken for accurate understanding or faithful human expression. A message may sound convincing while still omitting important context, introducing inaccuracies, or changing the sender's intended emphasis.</p>

<h2>Applying the Theory to Everyday Communication</h2>
<p>Consider two situations. In a professional setting, a salesperson gives AI three bullet points after meeting a potential customer: thank the customer for meeting, send the requested information, and suggest another conversation. The AI transforms those points into a polished email with formal phrases such as "I sincerely appreciate your valuable time." Although the message accurately communicates the salesperson's basic information, the customer may interpret its unusually formal tone as impersonal or automated.</p>

<p>In a personal setting, someone asks AI to improve a message to a landlord about a maintenance issue. The original request may be direct and frustrated, but AI might rewrite it as extremely polite and formal. The message still reaches the landlord, yet the revised wording could reduce the urgency the sender wanted to express. In both situations, successful delivery does not guarantee that the recipient understands the sender's intended tone or priorities.</p>
<p>These examples show that AI-assisted writing is not simply a matter of making communication faster. It can also change how personal effort and authenticity are communicated through language.</p>

<h2>Key Takeaway</h2>
<p><strong>A message can be technically perfect while still failing to communicate the sender's intended meaning or establish trust.</strong> Understanding AI-assisted communication therefore requires examining not only how messages are transmitted but also how people interpret them.</p>
